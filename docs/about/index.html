About The Regatta

The Regatta sells museum-grade prints of the age of sail at leisure and at speed — the clipper lithographs, the Cup’s first century, the luminist marines, the ship portraits — closing at the newly unlocked 1930 season. Every piece names its vessel, its builder or firm where known, and the water. One commission: THE BURGEE.

The doctrine

Rights. Everything on these walls was created or published before 1931 and is public domain in the United States; the 1930 season cleared in January 2026 under that rule. Each piece carries its source institution and locator, verified at acquisition. The rest of the J-class decade unlocks annually from January 2027 — the watch list is wired.

Marks. The historic Cup’s name and the great clubs’ burgees are held marks. They appear here only inside period artworks, as the historical documents they are — never as branding, and never in the Burgee commission, which the form itself refuses.

People. Victorian and Edwardian yachtsmen publish clean. Jazz Age figures with active estates carry a review flag, and the house honors it before any use beyond the historical record.

The named archives. The celebrated marine-photography firms’ archives are actively licensed. Their named work is sign-off-or-shelve; the house shelves it, and the 1930 wing runs on the anonymous press record instead.

The family

The Regatta is a wing of the family portfolio — smaller than The Paddock, higher on the AOV. Next door: Cartographer’s Dream (the chart), Painted Earth (your home water as a period chart), The Links (the other blazer).
